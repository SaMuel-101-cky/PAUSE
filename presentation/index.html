Beyond Instance-Wise Early Classification: Adaptive EEG Acquisition with Stopping-Dependent History for Sleep Staging

PAUSE (Prefix-Adaptive and Utility-Aware Stopping) decides how much single-channel EEG to acquire per 30 s sleep epoch. Where it stops determines both the current prediction and the historical context carried to future epochs.

PAGE 01 / 04 · THE SENSING BOTTLENECK

Model compression leaves the wearable EEG front end on

Continuous sleep staging assigns one of five AASM stages (W, N1, N2, N3, REM) to consecutive 30 s epochs of 100 Hz single-channel EEG. Because battery-powered sensor nodes acquire and transmit data independently of the host performing inference, compressing the classifier alone leaves sensing power untouched.

  • Compact 30 s classifiers still sense continuously: TinySleepNet and Micro SleepNet shrink neural compute on the host, yet demand complete 30 s inputs—keeping the Analog Front-End (AFE) powered 30/30 s (e.g., ADS1299-4 draws 22.0 mW active vs. 5.1 mW standby).
  • Offline masking blocks closed-loop control: MASS cuts nominal observation ratios via random masks, but relies on bidirectional future context across 32-epoch blocks (960 s), incurring a 495 s mean label-availability delay (30–960 s).
  • Online prefix stopping puts the front end to sleep: Acquiring a contiguous causal prefix and issuing a stop command at τn moves the AFE into standby while emitting the stage label immediately.

PAGE 02 / 04 · DEPENDENT SLEEP STREAMS

Conventional ECTS treats dependent sleep epochs as independent

Early Classification of Time Series (ECTS) provides a natural starting point for adaptive acquisition, yet its standard formulation treats successive series–label pairs as i.i.d. instances evaluated in isolation.

EQ. (1) · INSTANCE-WISE ECTS On,kinst = Xn,1:k

Stopping times and observations from earlier epochs 1..n-1 never enter the current prediction.

  • Cross-epoch dependence matters: Sleep stages unfold along structured physiological transitions where prior epochs anchor ambiguous patterns.
  • Minority stages suffer most: Transitional stage N1 accounts for only 3.2%–12.5% of epochs across the four evaluated datasets. Because N1 is a brief transitional sleep-onset stage lacking strong local waveform markers and REM’s single-channel EEG overlaps with W and N1, short prefixes depend heavily on prior-epoch transition context.
  • Instance-wise baselines stall: Methods like EARLIEST, ECONOMY-γ-Max, EarlyMix, and FIRMBOUND either drop N1 accuracy or require 23.7–29.8 s of EEG at their peaks.

PAGE 03 / 04 · STOPPING CHANGES HISTORY

Unacquired suffixes vanish from history and shift deployment

When an adaptive policy stops acquiring epoch m at prefix length τm, the remaining suffix Xm,τm+1:P is never recorded. Later epochs can condition only on the prefixes that were actually acquired.

EQS. (2)–(3) · STOPPING-DEPENDENT OBSERVATION Hn(τ<n) = (X1,1:τ1, …, Xn−1,1:τn−1) On,k = (Hn(τ<n), Xn,1:k)
  • Challenge 1 — Causal variable-length memory: How to compress variable-length prior prefixes 1..τm into a compact memory without leaking unacquired suffixes or recomputing past epochs at every patch.
  • Challenge 2 — Policy-induced history shift: A backbone trained solely on complete 30 s histories (τm = 30) suffers a covariate shift at deployment when given stopper-truncated histories across all eight cost settings (mean E[τ] ≈ 1.1–21 s), losing 0.89–12.02 macro-F1 pp across datasets and costs.

PAGE 04 / 04 · THE PAUSE FRAMEWORK

Three coupled mechanisms unify sensing, memory, and stopping

1 · CAUSAL ENDPOINT-MEMORY BACKBONE

Converts 1 s patches to PSD100 (128-band log-periodogram, 0.39–50 Hz), embeds via a gated SwiGLU MLP and left-causal conv stem, and applies a 7-block causal Transformer with 2D RoPE (patch & epoch axes) retaining 1 KV pair per epoch per layer across all 7 blocks (434 KiB total attention cache including the 30-patch local cache over 32 epochs). Trained with prior-free class-balanced LSEL (Log-Sum-Exp Loss) so majority stages do not dominate.

2 · UTILITY-AWARE FINITE-HORIZON STOPPER

Decouples evidence learning from calibration by scaling prior-free logits via per-prefix temperature Tk before adding the outer empirical prior log π̂—keeping rare stages un-suppressed during feature learning while restoring calibrated posteriors under deployment priors. Scores stop risk via misclassification matrix Λc,a = ρ wc I[c ≠ a] (ρ = 10, tempered class exponent α = 0.5), and estimates continuation risk via 29 sparse variational GPs (k = 1..29; stopping is forced at k = 30).

3 · PAIRED-HISTORY TRAINING

Pairs each window’s full history (τF = P = 30) with independently sampled random endpoint histories (τR ~ Unif{1..P}), preserving dense supervision across all 30 current-epoch prefixes to adapt the backbone to policy-induced context.

AT EACH PATCH

Compare calibrated risks

Prior-free scores are scaled by per-prefix temperatures Tk before adding outer prior log π̂, keeping rare stages un-suppressed during feature learning while restoring calibrated posteriors under deployment priors. Stop risk uses Λc,a = ρ wc I[c ≠ a] (ρ = 10, tempered class exponent α = 0.5); continue risk adds η to the predictive mean of 29 sparse variational GPs (k = 1..29; stopping is forced at k = 30).

BETWEEN EPOCHS

Carry 1 KV pair per endpoint

Causal attention with 2D RoPE (patch and epoch axes) over 1 s PSD100 (128-band log-periodogram, 0.39–50 Hz) tokens attends to the current prefix and 1 KV pair per epoch per layer across all 7 blocks (434 KiB total attention cache including the 30-patch local cache over 32 epochs). Skipped suffixes and future epochs are never seen.

DURING TRAINING

Pair full & random histories

Each window pairs complete histories (τF = 30) with independently truncated histories (τR ~ Unif{1..30}), optimizing dense prior-free class-balanced LSEL (Log-Sum-Exp Loss) across all 30 current prefixes to counter policy-induced history shift.

Acquired EEG
Reported seconds count observed 100 Hz signal per 30 s epoch. PAUSE surpasses all baseline peaks at 3.62–4.94 s, reducing acquisition by 6.0–6.8× versus each strongest baseline’s peak (23.67–29.76 s) and leaving 83.53%–87.93% of each 30 s epoch unacquired.
Front-end power
Analog front-end savings are analytically modeled from datasheet active and standby powers (ADS1299-4: 22.0 / 5.1 mW; ADS131A04: 15.8 / 2.6 mW; ADS1294: 10.1 / 4.0 mW) plus standby ratios, neglecting 0.002–0.11 mW wake-up recovery. Standby already comprises 54.5%–84.5% of PAUSE’s remaining AFE power; these figures do not measure end-to-end wearable battery life.
Inference latency
On a Dimensity 9500 mobile host (ONNX Runtime 1.28, single CPU thread, FP32, thermal steady state), 1 Hz scheduler-assigned patch inference took 9.44 ms median and 18.80 ms p99 (with 1.1%–1.3% stopper overhead in back-to-back runs). This is compute time per patch, distinct from physical EEG acquisition duration.
Evaluation scope
All evaluations use fixed subject-wise 20-fold partitions across four public PSG cohorts (20,242 + 40,293 + 162,456 + 324,148 = 547,139 pooled epochs in 32-epoch causal inference blocks), evaluating causal prefix stopping on recorded datasets alongside datasheet AFE power modeling and mobile CPU latency measurement rather than end-to-end wearable hardware trials.
